## Hot tips

### MAD LOVE ★

[Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/MAD%20LOVE/@59.909548,10.767964,18z) · `59.90955, 10.76796`

If you’re craving pizza in Oslo, Mad Love is the spot. They serve big New York–style slices and classic Neapolitan pizzas made with quality ingredients. The vibe is relaxed and friendly—perfect for a casual dinner or sharing one of their huge 52 cm pies with friends.

### Vaterland Bar & Scene ★

[Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/Vaterland%20Bar%20%26%20Scene/@59.913941,10.756161,18z) · `59.91394, 10.75616`

Don’t be intimidated by the heavy metal vibe from the outside. Inside, the staff recommended four different pizzas:
- Satan
- Judas
- Favan
- Biff & BærNEKRO

Make sure to ask for their signature spicy sauce! Just a warning: the heat kicks in about 20 seconds later. It’s a great spicy sauce that brings flavor without overwhelming it.

My favorite pizzas were definitely Satan and Judas.

We paid 849 NOK in total for 4 pizzas.

### Tabuno ★

[Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/Tabuno/@59.906757,10.760069,18z) · `59.90676, 10.76007`

Oslo has a new fine dining spot, and it’s Filipino. Hidden on a side street in Bjørvika, Tabuno offers a set menu starting at 1,200 NOK, with details and service that feel Michelin-level.

Highlights for me:
- Chicken & coconut broth
- King crab snacks with a spicy kick
- Hamachi with rice cake
- Skewers with egg (the most fun dish of the night)
- Duck with adobo sauce – crispy skin, juicy meat
- Pandan ice cream with strawberries to finish

Some dishes leaned a bit heavy or salty, but overall the presentation and experience were impressive.

💡 Pro tip: Let the staff guide your wine choices. Pairings make a big difference here.

Price ended at about 1,755 NOK per person with champagne and wine.

Definitely curious to see how the menu evolves.

### Cru Wine & French Food ★

[Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/Cru%20Wine%20%26%20French%20Food/@59.909130,10.754745,18z) · `59.90913, 10.75475`

Cru in Oslo is a cozy yet elegant restaurant blending French bistro flavors with Nordic ingredients, renowned for its refined dishes and one of the city’s best French-focused wine lists.

### Varemottaket ★

[Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/Varemottaket/@59.907250,10.758997,18z) · `59.90725, 10.75900`

Varemottaket is a tiny restaurant hidden behind a butcher shop. The menu is grilled over charcoal and changes often, served in a fun and lively setting with music and a casual vibe. It’s fine dining, but without the stiffness.

### Sorgenfri ★

[Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/Sorgenfri/@59.928153,10.719691,18z) · `59.92815, 10.71969`

Sorgenfri is part café, part gallery, and part design shop. It’s a creative space where you can sip a coffee or a glass of wine while browsing art, fashion, and books. The mix of old charm and modern style makes it one of Oslo’s most inspiring hangouts.

### Corrals Solli ★

[Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/Corrals%20Solli/@59.916496,10.715030,18z) · `59.91650, 10.71503`

For tacos with a kick, head to Corral’s. Their specialty is birria tacos—tender, slow-cooked beef served with a rich dipping broth. With locations around the city, it’s an easy pick when you’re in the mood for bold, flavorful street food.

### Restaurant Substans

[Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/Restaurant%20Substans/@59.911162,10.736135,18z) · `59.91116, 10.73613`

Restaurant Substans combines Nordic and Japanese flavors in a stunning glass setting by the waterfront. The menu is seasonal and creative, often served as a multi-course tasting experience. With its beautiful views and refined but relaxed vibe, it’s a great spot for a special night out in Oslo.

### Stranden 30

[Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/Stranden%2030/@59.908479,10.726007,18z) · `59.90848, 10.72601`

This spot has three distinct sections. There's a fine dining area where you can enjoy a full set menu, an outdoor section where you can order drinks and snacks using a QR code, and a more casual bistro offering a 3-course menu. Recommended dishes include the turbot with Szechuan-style seasoning and the steak with fries. This is currently the best restaurant at Aker Brygge

### SEVEN Oslo

[Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/SEVEN%20Oslo/@59.909186,10.754673,18z) · `59.90919, 10.75467`

SEVEN is the newest addition to Oslo’s dining scene, located inside the SIXA building in Barcode. While it sits within an office building, the moment you step inside, you’re transported to a different world. With a central cocktail bar and elegant interiors, SEVEN brings a level of luxury that feels new to this part of the city.

The space is designed by Kirsti, known for her work on other standout venues like Michaels. Her touch is clear in the warm lighting, thoughtful layout, and inviting atmosphere.

The kitchen offers a mix of Mediterranean and Asian flavors. On opening night, May 7, the team chose to keep bookings limited to focus on running a smooth first service and it paid off. The starters and snacks stood out, with a balanced mix of maki, sashimi, creamy hummus, and fresh homemade bread.

For the main courses, the Korean BBQ short ribs were a highlight. The dish brought together sour, spicy, umami, and sweet elements in a way that felt rich and satisfying. The lobster fettuccine was also a strong contender and showed off the kitchen’s versatility.

The current menu will likely see some adjustments over the coming weeks, but even now, it’s a strong start. For many, SEVEN may become a go-to spot for cocktails and shared plates rather than full meals. The cocktail bar itself is impressive and already stands above many in Oslo, both in execution and presentation.

SEVEN is also well-placed - just a five-minute walk from Oslo Central Station. Whether you’re planning a date, after-work drinks with colleagues, or dinner with friends, the space is flexible and welcoming. Live music and DJs bring extra energy to the evenings without overwhelming the dining experience.

With friendly staff, a great location, and prices that match most of Oslo’s dining scene, SEVEN has the potential to become a favorite for both locals and visitors.

## Michelin and bib gourmand ⭐️

## Fine Dining and Brasserie 🥩

### Brasserie - Vinland Brasserie

[Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/Brasserie%20-%20Vinland%20Brasserie/@59.914942,10.723653,18z) · `59.91494, 10.72365`

Vinland brings brasserie dining into one of Oslo’s most iconic buildings—the former U.S. Embassy designed by Eero Saarinen. Chef Charif El Messaoudi blends French, American, and Norwegian influences, serving bold dishes like grilled lobster, porterhouse steak, and seasonal seafood over an 800 °C broiler. Paired with one of Norway’s most celebrated wine lists, Vinland is both grand and approachable, perfect for a date night or celebration.

### Brasserie - Vintage Kitchen AS

[Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/Brasserie%20-%20Vintage%20Kitchen%20AS/@59.916962,10.762521,18z) · `59.91696, 10.76252`

Vintage Kitchen feels like a slice of Copenhagen in Oslo. By day, they serve open-faced Danish smørrebrød, and by night the place transforms into a stylish brasserie with small plates and an impressive wine list.

### Brasserie - Brasserie France

[Map](https://www.google.com/maps/search/Brasserie%20-%20Brasserie%20France/@59.913058,10.742803,18z) · `59.91306, 10.74280`
